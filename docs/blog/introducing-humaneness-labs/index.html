Who we are

A new group within LAION.

Humaneness Labs is a research group within LAION, the non-profit open AI research network. We study the behavior layer for AI: models of attention, emotion, perception, and social context that make interaction feel present.

We work the way LAION has always worked — in the open. Models, code, and datasets are shared so that others can test them, question them, and build on them.

Why listening comes first

Why listening comes first

Before a machine can respond as if it were present, it has to notice what people actually do: the hesitation before an answer, the laugh that breaks a sentence, the breath that says more than the words.

Voice reveals far more than words. Timing, breath, rhythm, effort, hesitation, and tiny shifts in articulation all expose what is happening beneath the surface. That is why our research starts here.

Humaneness Ears is the perception layer: it turns a recording into a rich, structured description of how something was said. It does not transcribe, caption, or generate speech.

      01 · Hear

      A recording becomes a picture of sound.

      Ears takes 0.1 to 30 seconds of audio. Stereo is averaged to mono, the signal is resampled to 16 kHz, and a Whisper frontend turns it into 80-bin log-mel features.

      • 0.1–30 seconds
      • 16 kHz mono
      • 80-bin log-mel

      02 · Encode

      A Whisper encoder, without the words.

      Base builds on a Whisper Base encoder, Medium on a Whisper Small encoder, both fully fine-tuned. There is no text decoder: every transformer layer contributes its own summary, and a learned gate mixes them for each recording.

      • Base · 6 layers · 26.4M
      • Medium · 12 layers · 97.1M
      • No text decoder

      03 · Feel

      Forty emotions at once, not one label.

      Ears predicts the intensity of all 40 EmoNet emotions simultaneously, on a 0–4 reference scale. A voice can be amused and embarrassed at the same time. A separate head rates perceived genuineness from 0 to 6.

      • 40 EmoNet emotions
      • Intensity 0–4
      • Genuineness 0–6

      04 · Describe

      How it was said, in 192 numbers.

      Next to emotion come 57 VoiceNet dimensions of delivery, register, timbre, and style, plus sound-quality and attribute scores: 192 scalars from one pass. Separate heads estimate speaking rate and two speaker-characteristic vectors.

      • 192 scores
      • 57 VoiceNet dimensions
      • 128D + 250D vectors

      The identity-style vector is not a person’s name or an identification guarantee.

      05 · Locate

      Find the laugh, the sigh, the gasp.

      Every 20 milliseconds, Ears estimates whether a vocal burst is happening. It proposes events with a start and an end, up to 32 per clip, and gives each one its three most likely labels from 53 classes.

      • 20 ms frames
      • Start + end per event
      • 53 burst classes

      Measured, honestly

      What it gets right, and where it doesn’t.

      On public human-label benchmarks, both models agree moderately with people. Medium is slightly ahead on emotion intensity; the two are close elsewhere. Neither is one universal best model.

      • Not a transcription or captioning model.
      • Training targets are machine annotations, not new human ratings.
      • Quality scores imitate teacher models rather than measuring human opinion.
      • Speaker vectors measure agreement with a teacher, not identity.
      • Clips over 30 seconds and multiple speakers need extra handling.
      BaseMedium

      CREMA-D and RAVDESS use small supervised adapters trained on the model’s 192 scores with actor cross-validation, not zero-shot predictions. The full benchmark report compares every target and baseline.
