Beyond a single emotional label

A performance can turn within a sentence.

Awe can soften into tenderness. A polite reply can fracture into hurt, then settle into resolve. These transitions are what we want to explore with Humaneness Voice.

Version 1.3 Alpha generates speech from literal text and English performance directions, with an optional separate voice reference. Its training covers English, German, French, Dutch, and Spanish. The examples below explore four of those languages.

This release continues from the 1.2 Alpha checkpoint and trains both the semantic backbone and the local talker. It is a full model checkpoint; the linked listening page also includes separate, later reinforcement-learning experiments.

Inside the model

From an idea to a waveform.

A large backbone carries meaning. A small talker handles the acoustic detail. Follow the five stages, or play the walkthrough to see the sequence unfold.

01 · Direction

Start with what the voice should mean.

The input combines the words to say with English performance directions: scene intent, emotional movement, pauses, and delivery. A separate speaker reference can guide voice identity.

Text and performance direction are distinct inputs. A voice reference is optional.

An illustrative direction
“Wait. Look at the sky.”
Breathless aweThen quiet tenderness
Words + context + optional voice reference

02 · Meaning

A large backbone carries the context.

A 36-layer Transformer builds the representation that conditions speech generation. The same context can carry literal words, pronunciation cues, and expressive intent.

Hidden size 2,560 · 32 attention heads · 8 key/value heads · context up to 32,768 tokens.

36 Transformer layers
Global semantic planning

03 · Talker

A small talker makes local decisions.

A compact, one-layer autoregressive Transformer turns the backbone’s representation into acoustic codes. It predicts the twelve codebook entries for each frame in sequence.

The local talker has hidden size 2,560 and 32 attention heads. Each codebook has 1,024 possible entries.

One local Transformer layer
Semantic context ↓Local acoustic generation ↓C₁ → C₂ → … → C₁₂
Meaning becomes acoustic choices

04 · Frames

Twelve codes. One acoustic frame.

The twelve code indices together represent 80 milliseconds of audio. At 12.5 frames per second, the model makes 150 codec-index decisions per second.

The animated cells illustrate prediction order; their colors are not actual codec values.

05 · Speech

Decode the sequence into sound.

The separate, frozen MOSS Audio Tokenizer v2 decoder converts the generated codes into a 48 kHz mono waveform. This is the point where the acoustic representation becomes something you can hear.

The release has approximately 4.13 billion parameters, excluding the external codec.

The training approach

Synthetic performances.
Permissively licensed real speech.

The training mix combines synthetic acting data with real speech licensed permissively, including for commercial use. Synthetic performances let us construct scenes and deliberately vary delivery; real speech contributes examples of human vocal behavior.

Performance directions separate the overall scene intention from local, script-aligned beats. This gives the model context for how an utterance develops, rather than reducing the whole clip to one emotion tag.
