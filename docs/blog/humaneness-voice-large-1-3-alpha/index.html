Beyond a single emotional label

A performance can turn within a sentence.

Awe can soften into tenderness. A polite reply can fracture into hurt, then settle into resolve. These transitions are what we want to explore with Humaneness Voice.

Version 1.3 Alpha generates speech from literal text and English performance directions, with an optional separate voice reference. Its training covers English, German, French, Dutch, and Spanish.

This release continues from the 1.2 Alpha checkpoint and trains both the semantic backbone and the local talker. It is a full model checkpoint; the linked listening page also includes separate, later reinforcement-learning experiments.

Inside the model

From an idea to a waveform.

A large backbone for meaning. A small talker for sound. Twelve acoustic codes at a time. Keep scrolling — the model assembles itself, stage by stage.

01 · Direction

Start with what the voice should mean.

The input combines the words to say with English performance directions: scene intent, emotional movement, pauses, and delivery. A separate speaker reference can guide voice identity.

  • Literal text
  • Performance direction
  • Voice reference · optional

02 · Meaning

A large backbone carries the context.

A 36-layer Transformer builds the representation that conditions speech generation. The same context carries literal words, pronunciation cues, and expressive intent — what should be said, and how it should feel.

  • 36 layers
  • Hidden size 2,560
  • 32 attention heads · 8 KV heads
  • Context up to 32,768 tokens

03 · Talker

A small talker makes local decisions.

A compact, one-layer autoregressive Transformer turns the backbone’s representation into acoustic codes. For every frame it predicts twelve codebook entries in sequence, each conditioned on the ones before.

  • 1 Transformer layer
  • Hidden size 2,560 · 32 heads
  • 12 codebooks × 1,024 entries

04 · Frames

Twelve codes. One acoustic frame.

Together, the twelve code indices describe 80 milliseconds of audio. At 12.5 frames per second, the model makes 150 codec decisions every second — tiny choices that add up to rhythm, articulation, and vocal expression.

  • 12 codes / frame
  • 80 ms / frame
  • 12.5 frames / s
  • 150 decisions / s

Cell colours illustrate prediction order, not actual codec values.

05 · Speech

Decode the sequence into sound.

The separate, frozen MOSS Audio Tokenizer v2 decoder converts the generated codes into a 48 kHz mono waveform. This is where the representation becomes something you can hear.

  • MOSS Audio Tokenizer v2
  • 48 kHz mono
  • ≈4.13B parameters, excluding the codec

The training approach

Synthetic performances. Permissively licensed real speech.

The training mix combines synthetic acting data with real speech licensed permissively, including for commercial use. Synthetic performances let us construct scenes and deliberately vary delivery; real speech contributes examples of human vocal behavior.

Performance directions separate the overall scene intention from local, script-aligned beats. This gives the model context for how an utterance develops, rather than reducing the whole clip to one emotion tag.
